## What is NIP-05?

NIP-05 (Nostr Implementation Possibility #5) lets you have a human-readable identifier like `you@domain.com` instead of a long string of random characters.

### The Problem It Solves

<DiagramCompare
  panels={[
    {
      title: "Before NIP-05",
      lines: ['Follow: npub1xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx', '"Hey, follow me! I\'m npub1abc123…xyz789"'],
    },
    {
      title: "After NIP-05",
      tone: "positive",

    },
  ]}
  caption="The same identity, in a form a human can actually repeat out loud."
/>

### What You Get

An address a person can pass on. "Find me at bob@nostrplebs.com" survives being
said out loud at a gig, printed on a card, or read at the end of a podcast
episode. It looks chosen rather than generated, most clients let people search
for you by it, and many of them put a small check next to it. That check is
worth understanding before you lean on it, and the Trust section below explains
exactly what it does and does not prove.

### But Remember...

> ⚠️ **NIP-05 is optional.** You can use Nostr perfectly well with just your npub.

The NIP-05 identifier is a **convenience**, not a requirement. Your `npub` is your real identity on Nostr.

## Why Get a NIP-05?

### Easier to Share

```
At a party:
"What's your Nostr?"

Without NIP-05:
"Uh... n-p-u-b-one-x-x-x... let me find it in my phone"

With NIP-05:
"Just alice@example.com"
```

### Professional Appearance

- **Businesses**: `support@yourcompany.com`
- **Creators**: `artist@yourdomain.com`
- **Developers**: `dev@opensourceproject.com`

### Trust & Authenticity

Many clients show a ✓ badge for NIP-05 users:

<MockProfile
  name="Alice"
  handle="alice@example.com"
  bio="Bitcoin educator"
  verified
  caption="A verified NIP-05 address shown the way most clients render it."
/>

That badge proves less than it looks like it proves. All it says is that the
domain in the address hands out that particular key for that particular name.
Everything else depends on the domain. `alice@alice.dev` carries weight because
Alice owns alice.dev and nobody else can publish anything there. `alice@` an
open free provider only means the name was unclaimed when somebody asked for
it, and an impostor who signs up for `alicee@` the same provider gets an
identical check next to their name.

So the question to ask is which domain the badge points at. If you recognise
the domain as the person's own, the check confirms the key belongs to them. If
the domain is one you have never heard of, the check tells you nothing about
who is behind the account.

### Do You Need One?

**No.** You can use Nostr perfectly fine without it.

**But...** it's highly recommended if you're:

- Building a personal brand
- Creating content
- Running a business
- Serious about Nostr long-term
- Tired of reading 63-character strings

### Not Centralization!

It is a fair worry. If your name lives on somebody else's domain, have you
handed them your identity? You have not. The address is a pointer; the npub is
what people actually follow. You can hold several addresses at once, let one
lapse, or move to your own domain later, and your followers stay where they
are, because what they followed was the key. Lose the address altogether and
you lose a convenience. Your account, your posts and your followers are
untouched.

## Using Your Own Domain

For complete control and professionalism, use your own domain.

### Step 1: Buy a Domain

Any domain registrar works:

- [Namecheap](https://namecheap.com)
- [Porkbun](https://porkbun.com)
- [Cloudflare Registrar](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/)

Cost: Usually $10-15/year

Owning the domain is only half of it. You also need somewhere that serves files
for that domain, which in practice means the web hosting your site already runs
on. Some site builders (Squarespace, Wix, Substack and similar) will not let
you place a file in a folder named `.well-known`, and there is no way around it
from inside their editor. If yours is one of those, take a free or paid
provider instead; the address works exactly the same way for the people
reading you.

### Step 2: Create the nostr.json File

Host a file at `https://yourdomain.com/.well-known/nostr.json`:

```json
{
  "names": {
    "alice": "b0635d6a9851d3aed0cd6c495b282167acf761729078d975fc341b22650b07b9"
  }
}
```

Your identifier becomes: `alice@yourdomain.com`

<Callout variant="warning" title="Use your hex public key, not your npub">
  NIP-05 requires the raw lowercase-hex form of your public key. If you paste
  `npub1...` into `nostr.json`, clients will fail to verify you. They fail
  silently too, with no message explaining why.

Some clients show the hex form somewhere in their key settings. If yours only
ever shows you `npub1...`, paste that into the checker further down this page,
which displays both forms side by side.

</Callout>

**No DNS records are involved.** NIP-05 verification is an ordinary HTTPS
request to `/.well-known/nostr.json`. You do not need a TXT record, a CNAME, or
any DNS entry beyond whatever already points your domain at your web host.

Two server settings decide whether web clients can read that file at all, and
both fail quietly when they are wrong. The file has to be sent with the header
`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, which is what lets a client running in a
browser read it; on most hosts this is one line of configuration, and support
will add it for you if you ask in exactly those words. The address also has to
answer for itself. If `yourdomain.com` bounces visitors to
`www.yourdomain.com`, verification breaks, because clients are required to
ignore redirects on this file. Whichever of the two you put in your NIP-05
address, that is the one that must serve the file directly.

### Example Setup Walkthrough

**Scenario:** You own `alice.dev`

1. **Create the JSON file:**

```json
{
  "names": {
    "alice": "b0635d6a9851d3aed0cd6c495b282167acf761729078d975fc341b22650b07b9"
  }
}
```

2. **Upload to your server:**

```
https://alice.dev/.well-known/nostr.json
```

3. **Verify it works:**
   Open in browser: `https://alice.dev/.well-known/nostr.json`

4. **Add to your Nostr profile:**
   In your client: Settings → Profile → NIP-05 → `alice@alice.dev`

### Troubleshooting Common Issues

Start with the checker above. Paste `you@yourdomain.com` into it and it will
tell you what a client sees when it looks you up. The lists below cover what to
do about the answers it gives.

**"NIP-05 not working"**

- **Check the key format first.** `nostr.json` must contain your public key in
  lowercase hex: 64 characters, no `npub1` prefix. An `npub` here is the single
  most common cause of a NIP-05 that never verifies.
- Ensure the JSON is valid (use a JSON validator)
- Check that the file is accessible at the exact URL

**"File not found"**

- Make sure `.well-known` folder is created
- Check your server serves hidden directories
- Verify the path: `/.well-known/nostr.json`

**"Domain not resolving"**

- DNS changes can take up to 24 hours
- Clear your DNS cache
- Optional, if you are comfortable at a terminal: `dig yourdomain.com` shows whether the name resolves yet

**"Client doesn't recognize it"**

- Some clients cache NIP-05 lookup results
- Try logging out and back in
- Wait a few minutes for propagation

**"Verification Failed" Error**

**Possible causes:**

1. **JSON syntax error** - Check for missing quotes or commas
2. **Wrong file location** - Must be at `/.well-known/nostr.json`
3. **Caching** - Wait 10-15 minutes and retry
4. **HTTPS required** - Must be served over HTTPS
5. **Missing CORS header** - Without `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on that file, clients running in a browser cannot read it, even though the file opens fine for you
6. **A redirect in the way** - If the address bounces from `yourdomain.com` to `www.yourdomain.com` or the other way round, clients ignore the redirect and give up

**Fix:**

- Validate JSON at jsonlint.com
- Check file is at exact path
- Clear client cache
- Ensure SSL certificate is valid
- Ask your host to send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` for that file
- Put the hostname that serves the file without redirecting into your NIP-05 address

**"Not Found" Error**

**Possible causes:**

1. File not uploaded to correct location
2. Server blocks dot-directories
3. Wrong domain

**Fix:**

- Verify file exists at exact URL
- Check `.htaccess` or server config allows dot-directories
- Confirm you're using correct domain

**Provider-Specific Issues**

**NostrPlebs:**

- Log into the management portal and check that the npub saved there matches yours exactly
- Ensure npub is correct (not nsec!)
- Contact support if stuck

**Self-hosted:**

- Run the checker above; it tells you whether the file is reachable and whether the key inside it is yours
- Ensure file permissions allow reading
- Check your server logs for errors, if you have access to them

<Callout variant="warning">
  Never share your nsec (private key) when setting up NIP-05. Only your npub
  (public key) is needed.
</Callout>

## Advanced Tips

### Multiple Identities

You can have multiple NIP-05 addresses:

```
personal@yourdomain.com
work@yourdomain.com
blog@yourdomain.com
```

All point to the same npub, or different ones.

### Subdomains

Use subdomains for organization:

```
alice@team.yourdomain.com
alice@community.yourdomain.com
```

### Hosting Multiple Users

You can host multiple NIP-05 identifiers on one domain:

```json
{
  "names": {
    "alice": "b0635d6a9851d3aed0cd6c495b282167acf761729078d975fc341b22650b07b9",
    "bob": "3bf0c63fcb93463407af97a5e5ee64fa883d107ef9e558472c4eb9aaaefa459d",
    "carol": "82341f882b6eabcd2ba7f1ef90aad961cf074af15b9ef44a09f9d2a8fbfbe6a2"
  }
}
```

### Verifying Your Setup

**Method 1: Your Client**

1. Add the NIP-05 to your profile
2. Save changes
3. Look for a checkmark ✓ or "verified" badge
4. May take a few minutes to propagate

**Method 2: Nostr Explorer**

1. Open `njump.me/yourname@yourdomain.com` in a browser
2. If your profile comes up, your NIP-05 resolves to your key

**Method 3: Direct Test (Command Line, optional)**

```bash
curl https://yourdomain.com/.well-known/nostr.json
```

**What Success Looks Like:**

- ✅ Client shows verification badge
- ✅ Others can find you by your NIP-05
- ✅ Your profile displays your human-readable name

### Privacy Considerations

A NIP-05 address ties your key to a domain, and a domain is something anyone
can look up. If it is registered in your own name, or the site sitting on it
already carries your bio and your photographs, the address quietly connects a
pseudonymous account to the rest of your life. Sometimes that is the whole
point. When it is not, keep the pseudonymous account on a free provider's
domain and save your own domain for the work you are happy to put your name to.
